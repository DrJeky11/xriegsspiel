# Supplied datasets: review and research integration

**Reviewed: 2026-09-15.** The four owner-supplied PDFs in [datasets](../datasets/README.md) add scenario background, facilitation methods, and empirical learning research. They complement the [game manual review](manual-review.md), which remains the source for edition-specific game mechanics. Product requirements remain as [confirmed by the owner](../product-requirements.md).

## Recommendation

Use the **education review and Red Team Handbook first** to improve the learning experiment: make assumptions visible, give each participant space to contribute, and evaluate reasoning and adaptation. Use the **two tactics publications as dated scenario-design references**, with explicit source and assumption records. Selecting a country-specific scenario, importing force data, and defining combat calculations remain separate design choices.

The useful addition is a traceable research foundation. A document collection does not by itself supply validated unit statistics, a game turn sequence, combat probabilities, or evidence that VR improves learning.

## 1. Chinese Tactics: background for dependencies and uncertainty

**Documented:** the preface describes a U.S. Army synthesis of Chinese doctrine, translated literature, and historical observations for training and professional education. It focuses on ground forces and tactical operations. It is not a Chinese-authored primary doctrinal publication or an observation of every unit's behavior. [D01](sources.md#d01), preface vii, PDF 11.

The selected passages connect task organization, command/support relationships, coordination, and information. Figure 4-1 shows a planning process with distinct outputs; paragraphs 4-11–4-13 discuss orders, coordination, and support. Paragraphs 6-2–6-5 describe reconnaissance as continuous. Appendix F describes communications development and limitations as understood in this 2021 edition. [D01](sources.md#d01), pp. 4-1–4-3, 6-1–6-2, F-1–F-2.

**Proposed use in XRiegsspiel:**

- Give a scenario designer vocabulary for dependencies among tasks, information, and support. In a fictional exercise, a delayed report or shared transport shortage can make a team reconsider its allocation.
- Distinguish a report's observation time from its delivery time and preserve the information available when an order was issued.
- Use the planning diagram as a comparison for briefing and staff-work outputs. It does not establish a universal game turn sequence, and it does not replace the owner's game-specific phases.

**Limits:** the file includes only the stated 2021 change, which adds a distribution number for printed copies. Its dated organization diagrams and modernization assessments do not establish 2026 force structure or capabilities. No equipment tables, performance values, maps, or national behavior model were imported.

## 2. Russian Tactics: planning context and support priorities

**Documented:** the preface similarly identifies a descriptive U.S. Army training publication. Its introduction explicitly distinguishes doctrinal descriptions from how Russian forces are fighting in Ukraine and warns that force diagrams and equipment assumptions may change. [D02](sources.md#d02), preface vii and introduction ix, PDF 11 and 13.

The selected planning table distinguishes commander direction, staff verification, final orders, acknowledgment, and updated estimates. Appendix H describes support priorities, storage, transport, maintenance, and requirements that depend on the mission and task organization. Appendix J discusses command-information systems. These are background descriptions; no reviewed passage supplies a complete XRiegsspiel adjudication model. [D02](sources.md#d02), table 3-1, p. 3-4; pp. H-1–H-2; p. J-1.

**Proposed use in XRiegsspiel:**

- Preserve orders, acknowledgments, and revised estimates as separate events where the selected scenario needs them.
- Make the consequences of competing support requests visible: a quantity in storage, capacity to move it, and delivery to a recipient can represent different state.
- Use the publication to ask instructors which abstractions serve the learning objective. For the initial fictional game, explicitly authored resource tokens are enough.

**Limits:** C1 corrects a Caspian Sea Flotilla figure; it is not a comprehensive update on the war. A force-comparison concept or a descriptive support norm must not silently become a combat probability, a universal consumption rate, or evidence of contemporary unit readiness. Country-specific background should remain separate from verified current observations and authored game rules.

## 3. Red Team Handbook: methods for the full planning cycle

**Documented:** the handbook's definition concerns structured critical thinking, alternative perspectives, and challenging assumptions. Its preface explicitly says its contents and courses are not official doctrine. This use of “red teaming” is broader than playing the opposing side in a game. [D03](sources.md#d03), preface ix (PDF 11), pp. 3–4 (PDF 17–18).

The four methods below were read in their entirety. The proposed product uses are original adaptations, not claims of tested effectiveness in XRiegsspiel.

| Method and source locator | Documented purpose | Proposed use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Think-Write-Share, pp. 201–202 (PDF 215–216) | Develop individual ideas before methodical group sharing | Private draft responses, followed by deliberate team sharing; browser and Quest participants have equal opportunity to contribute. |
| Key Assumptions Check, pp. 163–165 (PDF 177–179) | Identify premises, confidence, dependencies, and conditions that undermine them | Attach an assumption, supporting report, confidence explanation, and reconsideration trigger to a decision. |
| Premortem Analysis, pp. 173–174 (PDF 187–188) | Imagine a plan has failed, identify possible causes individually, then revise the plan | A short facilitator prompt before commitment; retain the original plan and the revision for discussion. |
| 4 Ways of Seeing, pp. 79–80 (PDF 93–94), table 7.2 | Compare each stakeholder's view of itself and the other | A perspective worksheet with evidence/uncertainty labels. Treat statements about others as hypotheses, not hidden truths revealed by the software. |

Chapter 5 discusses hierarchy, private reflection, and unattributed contributions as ways to address group dynamics (pp. 51–54). **Design implication:** if shared contributions omit names, explain who can still inspect authorship; do not promise anonymity while facilitator logs identify writers. A reflection draft, team briefing, and referee record need different visibility rules.

Keep **challenging a plan** separate from **contesting an adjudicated result**. The first belongs in team planning and reflection; the second follows the confirmed R5 referee workflow. An assumption-check prompt should not automatically pause play, change the rules, or grant access to an opponent's information.

## 4. Educational Wargaming: strengthen the evidence for learning

**Documented:** Bennerstedt and Sjöblom present a semi-systematic review of fifteen studies described as empirical, covering 2014–2024. It is a literature synthesis, with two explicitly discussed boundary cases based on practitioner/secondary material, rather than fifteen equivalent controlled trials. Method and selection details appear on printed pp. 246–248 (PDF 7–9). [D04](sources.md#d04)

The article adapts the **Presage–Process–Product (3P)** framework: learning conditions, what participants do, and what they learn. It warns against treating engagement, success within the game, or self-report alone as evidence of professional competence. It identifies limited systematic examination of retention/transfer and of how facilitation and debriefing produce learning. See figure 1, p. 244 (PDF 5); assessment discussion, pp. 252–253 (PDF 13–14); conclusions, p. 256 (PDF 17). [D04](sources.md#d04)

**Proposed application to the existing evaluation plan:**

| Dimension | What to record | Why it matters for XRiegsspiel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conditions | Prior wargame/VR experience, scenario/rules versions, assigned roles, briefing, interface, facilitator prompts | A different briefing or more instructor help can explain a result attributed to the interface. |
| Process | Selected decisions, rationale, reports available, alternatives, team discussion observations, revisions, referee interventions | Valid orders and fast clicks alone cannot show whether the team reasoned about a tradeoff. |
| Outcomes | A short prior-knowledge task, an equivalent post-exercise task, and reasoning on a changed vignette; delayed follow-up if feasible | Separate rule fluency, perceived enjoyment, and the ability to apply a concept elsewhere. |

Use the existing proposal for counterbalanced browser/VR trials and equivalent scenarios. Preserve facilitation conditions and record deviations. A small pilot can demonstrate feasibility and usability; this article does not establish a Quest-specific benefit, validate automated adjudication, or supply a numeric success threshold for this project.

**Source-quality observations:** Table 2 says “2014–October 2024,” while the surrounding text says January–December 2024 and a last search update of 20 December 2024 (PDF 8). Preserve the inconsistency if reproducing the search. The article's 2026 publication date does not make its reviewed corpus extend through 2026.

**Useful follow-up material:** the supplied article lists three separate supplements on printed p. 257 (PDF 18). The publisher page also offers them. They are not embedded evidence tables in the local PDF and were not retrieved in this pass:

- [Appendix 1: study characteristics and stated learning objectives](https://doi.org/10.31374/sjms.451.s1).
- [Appendix 2: operationalization of learning and methodological transparency](https://doi.org/10.31374/sjms.451.s2).
- [Appendix 3: theoretical frameworks and integration](https://doi.org/10.31374/sjms.451.s3).

These are promising inputs for a future study comparison table. Read the original studies and available instruments before adopting their measurements; a secondary review cannot supply missing instruments or participant-level data.

## How to turn documents into usable research records

**Proposed workflow; no ingestion service or runtime dataset was built.**

1. Keep the four originals and use D01–D04 as stable source IDs. The [source register](sources.md#d01) pins editions and SHA-256 hashes.
2. Extract only the passages needed for a research question, preserving page boundaries and printed locators. Review figures and tables visually before representing them as structured records.
3. For each research note, keep: source ID/hash, printed and PDF page, section/paragraph, paraphrased claim, evidence type, date/scope limits, intended product use, and review status.
4. For an authored scenario parameter, additionally keep: value and units, designer rationale, related source note, rules/scenario version, and whether it has been instructor-reviewed. A source passage can motivate a parameter without validating its numeric value.
5. If searchable document assistance is added later, retrieve by document/section with citations. Keep facilitator notes and side-specific scenario information within their existing visibility boundaries. Retrieved prose must not become executable rules or a referee ruling automatically.

**Observed extraction issue:** Poppler extracted text from all four files, but some ATP dates and Russian transliterations contain incorrect glyphs. The Russian PDF metadata title is merely “Chapter 1.” Publication titles and dates were checked against the pages, including rendered change sheets; metadata alone is insufficient. Diagrams can contain information absent from extracted text.

## Smallest useful next experiment

This is a proposed extension of **Island Coordination**, not an approved scenario or a new requirement:

1. Brief a fictional team with competing support requests and incomplete reports.
2. Ask each participant to record one important assumption privately, then share and choose one assumption to monitor.
3. Let the team allocate a scarce resource using the existing legal-action guidance and bookkeeping proposal.
4. Introduce a changed report or delivery delay. Preserve the original information, decision, and revised rationale.
5. Run the existing contest/referee demonstration separately, then review both the reasoning and the ruling.
6. Ask learners to explain the tradeoff in an equivalent changed vignette. Record facilitator help and interface friction separately from the reasoning assessment.

This exercises R2–R3 and R5–R9 without first requiring a national order of battle or detailed equipment models. Start with an instructor worksheet or a small set of prompts; implement additional planning UI only after the exercise shows what records are useful.
