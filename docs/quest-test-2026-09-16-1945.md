## Findings

### 1. First-time orientation is insufficient

**Owner report:** the player could operate the interface without understanding the purpose, mechanics or effect of the orders. The owner explicitly requests a way to teach a new player, or at minimum clearly publish the exercise's purpose.

**Source observation:** the mission is present in the AI home panel and a paginated **Briefing & rules** panel exists, but starting the match opens the action-oriented home screen. The interface does not guide the first planning/resolution cycle. CP, staff reports, cargo delivery and readiness require prior understanding. In MR, results are presented one event at a time; round 1 starts with deployment/round messages before the player's action outcomes, and advancing does not require understanding those outcomes.

**Proposed priority:** present a short scenario orientation with role, objective, map location, units and cargo, deadline and the first decision. Keep mission progress visible and offer a guided first round. Explain each resolved order and its effect on the objective before offering the next round. A teaching objective should be explicit and separate from the win condition; a proposed focus here is allocating limited actions between movement, protection and delivery.

For this run, Blue's game objective is **deliver at least three routine supply manifests by the end of round 6**. B1 and B2 carry the supply cargo. B3 is the response launch. Reaching the marked Sierra Madre transfer area does not deliver cargo: each manifest needs a later delivery action. Each round permits three command points and at most three orders; each vessel acts once. A movement order costs one CP and can cover up to four legal water hexes. Both sides' sealed plans resolve together.

### 2. The selected variant explains the restricted opening

**Database observation:** this was **Short window**, not a shorter total match or a difficulty setting. Both supply transports, B1 and B2, were unavailable during rounds 1–2. The historical action groups were B3 and Staff. Round 2 still offered both groups even though the submitted plan contained only one staff order. B1 and B2 become available in round 3.

**Source observation:** the chooser button says `Window: Short (rounds 3–6)`; the longer availability explanation is in the briefing. The home screen does not prominently explain why the transports cannot act.

**Proposed priority:** state “Supply ships arrive in round 3; six rounds total” before starting and beside unavailable ships. Explain one action per vessel, unused CP and other available groups at the decision point. Use Baseline for the guided first experience; it is already the default, so do not record a default-setting change as a fix.

### 3. Regional context and close-up play are disconnected

**Owner report:** starting the AI exercise appeared to remove the rest of the map. On returning to the larger Palawan/Spratlys map, the player expected to locate Second Thomas Shoal and the scenario's pieces there.

**Source observation:** `SPR-H01` is pinned to `palawan-spratlys/focus`. Starting/resuming it switches to that map. The focus is a 36 × 28 km board with 0.75 km hex spacing; the regional map is 960 × 690 km with 18 km spacing. These are separate boards, not continuous zoom levels of one exercise. The renderer replaces the terrain when switching, and scenario pieces are drawn only when the active map ID exactly matches the scenario. `focusCell()` deliberately does nothing during XR; the desktop focus call is not evidence of headset framing.

The switch to the smaller board explains the loss of regional context. It does **not** establish that no additional clipping, missing geometry or headset framing problem occurred; that needs a targeted headset reproduction.

**Proposed priority:** identify the close-up within its regional context, keep a visible active-exercise label and provide “Return to active exercise.” A regional locator/footprint and aggregated scenario markers could preserve orientation without copying units into a separate assembly or changing their authoritative movement grid. Exact marker behavior remains a design proposal.

### 4. Separate saved states look like unexplained piece changes

**Database observation:** the new empty exercise affected the regional assembly only. At review time, the Second Thomas Shoal assembly still held four pieces at revision 5; the regional assembly was empty at revision 17. The AI run holds another independent roster. Returning to **Map assembly** displays the saved assembly on the current map, not the AI roster.

This provides a concrete explanation for the apparent reappearance of pieces. There is no evidence in this trace of AI units being copied into the assembly. The narration alone cannot identify every piece the owner saw.

**Proposed priority:** explicitly name the mode and saved exercise, use distinct labels for “New map assembly” and “New AI scenario,” and explain what a mode/map switch will display. Preserve all six independent map saves and each AI run.

### 5. Reopening AI after a map change has a source-level defect

**Source observation:** `onMap(id)` hides an active opponent when the map differs. The public `activate` callback calls `show()` only; it does not call `displayMap()` for the retained run. `render()` then supplies an empty piece list when the map differs. Consequently, leaving the scenario for a regional assembly and clicking **Play against AI** can reopen an existing scenario menu over the wrong terrain with no scenario pieces.

This is a specific code-path finding consistent with the confusion; the owner's precise sequence was not independently reproduced in a browser or headset during this review.

**Proposed fix and check:** reopening a retained scenario must restore its map before rendering its units. Verify focus AI → regional assembly → Play against AI, in browser and MR, while preserving run ID, round, revision, unit positions and both map assemblies.

### 6. Loading felt slow; cause remains unmeasured

**Owner report:** initial loading was slow but acceptable for this trial.

**Source observation:** workspace initialization waits for the piece catalog, equipment reference data and multiple terrain datasets, then constructs map/rules and visual classifications. The restart build also reported the existing large frontend bundle warning. These are candidates for profiling, not a measured diagnosis.

**Proposed follow-up:** measure a cold Quest load by stage, add visible loading progress, and defer nonessential reference data if the measurements justify it. Do not infer load time or headset performance from desktop build time.

## Next acceptance check

Prioritize scenario orientation and map/mode continuity, then clear round summaries and loading measurements. A first-time player should be able to explain who they control, what constitutes delivery, how to win, why a ship is unavailable and what their last order accomplished. They should move between the regional context, active AI exercise and map assembly while knowing which state they are viewing. Recheck physical grip behavior during that flow; complete a match separately before claiming end-to-end AI headset acceptance.
